# Two Wrath of the Skies and one Pest Control is the wrong sweeper package

origin: pilot-experience
subject: Wrath of the Skies
subject: Pest Control
status: open

## Evidence sought

Two questions that have to be read together, which is why they share a record.
How many sweepers the 75 sides is the first: the current package is three
cards, and both two and three are live answers. Which cards they are is the
second, and the argument there is a third Wrath of the Skies against the Pest
Control that holds the slot now.

Read as one card each, the two subjects would answer separately and neither
answer would be about the package. What the claim is about is the sum and the
mix at once, so the camp's counts of both are the one reading.

The performance tilt on each configuration is what tells a camp that has
settled from a camp that has merely copied. A configuration the camp is on that
finishes behind the camp is the herd adopting an underperformer, and a
sideboard slot nobody argues about is exactly where that happens.

### 2026-08-08 judgment

Pest Control is a wrath effect. Sweeper density is read over Wrath of the Skies and Pest Control copies summed, wherever boarded; the package question is count and mix of the class, not of Wrath alone.

### 2026-08-11 judgment

Decided: in the current meta Pest Control is worse than Wrath of the Skies. Wrath is the more versatile catch-all and Pest Control does not answer what the field is presenting. Density, meaning how many sweepers the package holds in total, remains undecided. The reasoning given for the mix was that aggro decks are no longer as prevalent.

### 2026-08-11 data

Correction to the previous entry: aggro share is the wrong population for this record. Wrath is bought for Affinity, the worst matchup, and for Gruul Basking Broodscale. Those two are flat over the three 14-day snapshots, 6.8% to 7.4% to 7.2%, with Broodscale rising 2.3% to 3.0%. All Eldrazi 14.4% to 13.5%. The meta reading does not argue for cutting a sweeper. The camp is also consolidating on three: Wrath 0/3 went 72% to 89% (delta +0.17) while 0/2 shrank 16% to 11%, and 0/3 now sits one list from core. Draw math on the count, 60 cards with no mulligan and no digging: P(at least one seen) by turn 4 on the play is 30.8% at two copies against 42.7% at three, a 39% relative lift, and the lift holds near 40% across turns 1 to 6. What this does not price is how often the third copy is a redundant extra in hand, which only the match logs hold.

### 2026-08-11 data

Supersedes the population used in the two entries above. Wrath answers aggro and Affinity and Broodscale, so the denominator is their union, not any one of them. That union is 33.6% to 33.2% to 29.4% over the three 14-day snapshots: down 4.2 points, all of it from aggro, but still close to a third of the field. Read against the rest of the board, three Wrath for 29.4% is the same allocation rate as three Mystical Dispute for 34.2% of blue decks, and it is the second broadest card in the fifteen. By that measure the denser allocation is four Consign to Memory against a 10.4% mirror, though Consign answers more than the mirror and its share is understated here. Nothing in the field data argues for cutting to two. The remaining argument for two is the cost the field cannot see, namely how often the third copy is redundant in hand, which the match logs hold.
